# Style rules

<!-- Review note: Claude drafted this file from Nick's Nov 10, 2026 study of how Linear, Superhuman and The Browser Company write. None of it is Nick's own wording yet. Delete this comment once reviewed. -->

`SKILL.md` decides what to say. This file decides how it sounds on a given surface. When the two conflict, `SKILL.md` wins.

These rules are for human readers. For a spec, prompt, or skill, section 3 of `SKILL.md` governs instead.

The model: borrow the evidence habits of Linear, Superhuman and The Browser Company (numbers, plain verbs, candor backed by data). Skip their company confidence (superlatives, contrarian labels). From one person, it reads as unearned.

## Every surface

1. **Claim first, in ten words or fewer.** The summary's first sentence states the claim, the way Linear states its thesis lines: "We replaced purpose with metrics."
2. **A result carries a number, a unit and a time frame, or it goes.** Superhuman: product-market fit score "22%" in 2017, "58%" three quarters later. Use only numbers you can defend when someone asks how you measured them.
3. **Verb and object, few adjectives.** Plain is not vague: the verb still needs an object and a result.
4. **Methods as recipes.** Give the exact prompt, rubric, config, or question, not a summary of it. Superhuman published its survey question word for word.
5. **Ban list.** excited, thrilled, ever made, whopping, game-changer, revolutionary, exclamation marks, emoji in titles. Why: these are the weakest lines in the writing studied.

## Resume bullets

- Shape: `<verb> <object>, <result with number and unit>, <time frame>`. One line per bullet.
- No named enemy and no contrarian label. Why: from a job seeker, it reads as arrogance.

## Posts (blog, X, LinkedIn)

- Open on one line that holds the trade-off: "Technology makes it faster to build, but harder to care." Put the evidence in the next line, or the opener reads as a slogan.
- Short, then long: a short line lands the point, the next one explains it. Don't chain fragments.
- A named enemy only when it is a practice, not a person or a company, and at most once per post.
- Credit a metaphor to a famous name only if you got it from them yourself. Why: otherwise it reads as borrowed status.

## Retrospectives

- Put one mistake in its own heading ("What I got wrong"), before any defense.
- Back it with your own unflattering number, as The Browser Company did: "Only 5.52% of DAUs use more than one Space regularly."
- Write one only when there is a real lesson. Why: staged humility reads as a humble-brag.

## Release notes (Amplify, inknotes)

- One sentence: name, what it is, availability. Linear: "Diffs, Linear's fast and focused review experience, is now available for iOS and Android."
- No "we're excited", no exclamation marks.

## Limits

- All three are design-led, founder-voiced startups. This register suits startup AI engineer and FDE roles more than enterprise hiring.
- Voice can't stand in for evidence. Recruiters screen bullets for scope and results first.
